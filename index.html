Horizon Europe · TU Berlin · 2025–26 · with Mainflux Labs

A sensor-calibrated digital twin that fixes overheating in a low-tech office

Worked with Prof. Timo Hartmann (TU Berlin) · Klaus Luig (REINCARNATE) · Saša Klopanović (Mainflux Labs)

Problem

The 3L office in Menden, Germany heats and cools through a concrete-core TABS slab. It overheated in summer, and operators needed about 14 days of manual tuning to stabilise it.

Method

Generated an EnergyPlus model from the architectural drawings and IFC file, ran it with EnergyPlus weather data for the nearest station (Düsseldorf), tuned it against hourly sensor data, then wrote a forward-looking controller for the TABS slab.

Real data from the 3L office

Result

Calibrated to CV(RMSE) 9.65% (limit 30%), RMSE 2.34 °C, NMBE under 1%. Automated control cut peak temperature from 31.3 to 26.0 °C and the indoor temperature range by 67%. The model also reproduced indoor CO₂ within 5.3% of the measured average (NMBE), validating air quality as well as temperature.

Horizon 2020 · Building data analyst · 2022–24

Finding HVAC faults in operational data, located on BIM

Worked with Prof. Timo Hartmann (TU Berlin)

Problem

A working office produces thousands of sensor readings, and an outlier in a table gives facility staff no clue which room or component it came from.

Method

Flagged temperature anomalies against a moving average in R, then mapped them onto a clickable BIM model of the Kineum office in Unity.

Real data from the Kineum office · ASHVIN demo site

Result

100,000+ records analysed across 50+ zones; outliers placed on the 3D model, so it is clear which part of the building is off. Next step: checking live readings against a calibrated physics model, so a deviation points straight to the component at fault.

Horizon 2020 · TU Berlin · 2024

Will a 1961 duplex in Gdynia overheat as the climate warms?

Worked with Prof. Timo Hartmann (TU Berlin) · Nour Samaro

Problem

A 1961 brick duplex in Gdynia, Poland was never designed for hotter summers. How often will its living room overheat by 2050 and 2080, and does upgrading the envelope fix it?

Method

83 EnergyPlus runs on the existing building and an upgraded envelope, using historical and RCP 8.5 future weather files, with overheating judged by the ASHRAE 55 adaptive comfort model.

Real data from the Gdynia case study

Result

As built, the living room overheats for 73.5% of summer by 2061–80. Better windows (U 3.3 → 1.6), extra insulation and shading cut that from 2,700 to 638 hours, still above the limit, so the building will also need active cooling.
