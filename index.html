Horizon Europe · Master's thesis, TU Berlin · Feb 2026 · with Mainflux Labs

A sensor-calibrated digital twin that fixes overheating in a low-tech office

Thesis: An Analytical Framework Using Sensor Data to Evaluate the Energy Performance of Built Environments.

Worked with Prof. Timo Hartmann (TU Berlin, supervisor) · Klaus Luig (REINCARNATE, second examiner) · Saša Klopanović (Mainflux Labs, external advisor)

REINCARNATE

Problem

The 3L office in Menden, Germany ("low-tech, low-energy, low-maintenance") relies on a concrete-core TABS slab and natural ventilation. In practice it overheated, and its operators needed about 14 days of manual tuning to stabilise the slab.

Method

Python BIM-to-BEM from ArchiCAD IFC to EnergyPlus. Cleaned 3,281 hourly sensor records (robust z-score anomaly detection, 99.4% complete), then calibrated in three rounds to ASHRAE Guideline 14. Wrote an RMOT-based TABS controller that anticipates loads 24–48 h ahead.

Result

Calibrated to CV(RMSE) 9.65% (limit 30%), RMSE 2.34 °C, NMBE under 1%. Automated control cut peak temperature from 31.3 to 26.0 °C and the indoor temperature range by 67%, with the energy cost quantified (+39% site energy, mostly low-carbon district heat).

Heating system analysis

Plant
200 mm concrete TABS slab, ground-source heat pump, 2,000 L buffer, 60 °C district-heating loop
Signature
Heating-only operation: 5,745 kWh/yr district heat, peaking at 702 kWh in January; no active cooling
Radiant effect
Slab 25.1–31.4 °C; MRT 4.2 °C above air temperature in January, so comfort at lower air temperatures
Control
ISO 11855-based RMOT logic: 5,472 h heating, 3,288 h cooling, only 22 mode switches a year

Data partner: Mainflux Labs. Their IoT platform supplied the measured indoor temperature, humidity and CO₂ from the 3L building (AtmoCube sensors, April–September 2025) that I used to calibrate and validate the model. More on Mainflux ↓

Also within REINCARNATE: machine-learning crack detection for assessing brick quality, so masonry can be reused.

Horizon 2020 · TU Berlin · 2024 · weather data by Arup

Will a 1961 duplex in Gdynia overheat as the climate warms?

Worked with Prof. Timo Hartmann (TU Berlin) · Nour Samaro

BIM‑SPEED

Problem

A three-storey duplex from 1961 with 56 cm brick walls, in a cool-humid climate (ASHRAE 5A). It was not built for future heatwaves. How much will it overheat, and do envelope upgrades help?

Method

83 EnergyPlus runs, as built vs. upgraded envelope, under 2017–21 weather and RCP 8.5 projections for 2040 and 2070; ASHRAE 55 adaptive comfort.

Result

As built, the living room overheats for 73.5% of summer by 2061–80. Upgrades (window U-value 3.3 → 1.6, insulation, shading) cut that from 2,700 to 638 hours, still above the 200-hour limit, so active cooling is needed too.
